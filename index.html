      How to play

      1. Notice

      Something happens. Smell spreads out from where it started, so the people closest react first. Sniff (Q) to work out what it is. The smell tells you what they ate.

      2. Investigate

      Talk to people (E). Ask what they ate and where they were standing. Hold E next to a bag to search it. Stand close to whisperers to overhear them. One person is lying: when you find proof, show it to them and watch them crack.

      3. Stay unseen

      Odd behaviour costs Aura, but only if someone sees it. The eye under your Aura meter shows how many people can see you. Walking and polite questions are always free.

      4. Call it

      Before time runs out, press O. Early cases just need a name. Later ones need proof, and the final cases are a full cross-examination.

      What costs Aura (only if seen)

      Sniffing −6 · Searching a bag −18 (more if it is theirs) · Squeezing past someone −3 · Talking during silent vows −4 · An audible fart −30. Mentioning the smell first makes that person suspect you. Suspicion fades while you act normal.

      Somebody did something unforgivable in a small, crowded space. Work out who, then call them out in front of everyone, without anyone deciding it was you.

      Season One: Thornbury Tower One week, one elevator, one wedding

      Who Farted?

      A comedy deduction game. Someone farted at the worst possible moment. You work out who, and present the evidence in front of everyone, without anyone deciding it was you.

      Investigate

      The fart is a live gas simulation that rides the room's air currents. What someone ate decides how loud it was and how it smells, based on real gut science. Everyone tells the truth except the culprit. Overhear whispers, snoop bags, find the photo.

      Keep your aura

      Your reputation is the fail state. Everyone has a line of sight, some notice more than others, suspicion spreads as gossip, and playground law applies: smelt it, dealt it. You also need to go, so you are a suspect too.

      Prove it

      Everyone forms a theory, and you can plant rumours to steer the room. When it is time to call it, the accused testifies and you break each statement with evidence, Ace Attorney style.

      Why this, why now

      Fart games exist: Bad Air Day on iOS, the board game PRRRT..., and a multiplayer hidden-role game called Who Fart? listed on Steam. None of them is a detective game. Comedy deduction sells: the Frog Detective series has sold over 500,000 copies, and reviewers of Duck Detective: The Secret Salami mainly complained that it was too short.
